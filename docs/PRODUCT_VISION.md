# Gather HQs Product Vision

## Positioning

**Gather HQs is the digital headquarters for organizations.**

It is not limited to event registration. It combines a public website, people and membership management, events, communications, operations, payments, reporting, documents, and assisted workflows in one organization-owned workspace.

## Mission

Help small and midsize organizations operate professionally without assembling and maintaining a disconnected collection of website, event, CRM, email, task, form, volunteer, sponsor, and payment products.

## Primary customers

- Community and membership organizations
- Clubs and associations
- Recreation and sports programs
- Nonprofits
- Local event operators
- Professional groups
- Volunteer-led organizations
- Small businesses that run recurring events or member programs

## Core customer problems

- Public information is scattered across websites, social media, email, and spreadsheets.
- Contact and membership history is incomplete or owned by individual volunteers.
- Event registrations, invitations, attendance, waivers, tasks, sponsors, volunteers, and payments are disconnected.
- Organizations repeatedly recreate communications and operational checklists.
- Leadership lacks a reliable, organization-wide view of activity and performance.
- Staff and volunteers need simple tools, not enterprise software complexity.

## Product pillars

1. **Website** — Publish the organization's public presence, content, events, forms, and calls to action.
2. **People** — Maintain a durable CRM for contacts, members, households, volunteers, sponsors, and engagement history.
3. **Events** — Plan, publish, invite, register, attend, communicate, charge, and report.
4. **Operations** — Coordinate tasks, documents, forms, waivers, calendars, volunteers, and recurring work.
5. **Communications** — Send and track announcements, campaigns, invitations, reminders, and notifications.
6. **Finance** — Collect payments and expose practical financial operations and reporting.
7. **Insights** — Give leaders a clear view of organization health and activity.
8. **Automation and AI** — Reduce repetitive work while preserving human review and control.

## Product principles

### Simplicity before breadth

A new customer should be able to create an organization, brand a basic website, publish an event, and test registration quickly. Advanced modules should be progressively disclosed.

### The organization is the center

Events are important, but the long-lived organization, its people, records, communications, and workflows are the primary product boundary.

### One source of truth

People, events, payments, tasks, files, messages, forms, volunteer work, and sponsor relationships should connect instead of existing as isolated records.

### Secure multi-tenancy by default

Every organization-owned query, action, file, export, background task, webhook, and AI context must be tenant-scoped. Authentication alone is never sufficient authorization.

### Consistent interaction patterns

Lists, filters, forms, comments, attachments, status changes, audit history, permissions, and empty states should behave consistently across modules.

### Mobile usability is required

Core administrative and event-day workflows must work on phones and tablets. Mobile is not a later visual cleanup step.

### Human-controlled AI

AI may draft, summarize, classify, and suggest. It must not autonomously publish, send messages, modify permissions, charge/refund money, or make consequential changes without explicit approval.

### Operational transparency

Background work, communications, webhooks, automations, payments, and AI actions need visible status, error details, retries, and audit history.

### Build for real workflows

New features require a clear user, problem, trigger, workflow, and expected outcome. Avoid adding a module solely because competitors have it.

## Experience goals

- A first-time administrator understands what to do next without documentation.
- A public visitor can register or respond with minimal friction.
- A manager can see important changes and overdue work immediately.
- A contact has one coherent profile and activity history.
- Event-day staff can complete essential actions from a phone.
- Errors explain what happened and what the user can do next.
- Advanced power does not make the default interface intimidating.

## Record standards

Where relevant, organization records should support:

- Tenant ownership
- Role-based permissions
- Created/updated timestamps and actor attribution
- Activity or audit history
- Tags or categories
- Search and filtering
- Comments or notes
- Attachments
- Export where appropriate
- Archive/deactivate behavior instead of destructive deletion

These capabilities should be implemented intentionally, not forced onto records where they add no value.

## What Gather HQs should not become

- A full general-ledger accounting platform in the near term
- A generic enterprise CRM detached from organization and event workflows
- An unconstrained website-hosting environment that permits unsafe custom scripts
- A social network
- An autonomous AI operator
- A collection of unrelated modules with inconsistent navigation and permissions

## Commercial strategy

The strongest recurring value comes from becoming operational infrastructure. Pricing should reflect organization value and usage while keeping entry simple.

Potential plan differentiators:

- Number of staff/managers
- Contacts or active members
- Email/SMS volume
- Automation runs
- AI credits
- Storage
- Custom domains and white-labeling
- Advanced reports, API access, and SSO

Avoid pricing that penalizes healthy event attendance in a way that makes customers reluctant to grow.

## Success measures

- Time from signup to published website and first event
- Percentage of organizations completing a real registration test
- Weekly active organizations
- Number of modules meaningfully adopted
- Contact/member data growth and repeat engagement
- Event registration conversion and attendance
- Communication deliverability
- Customer retention and expansion
- Support requests per active organization
- Reliability of payments, notifications, automations, and background processing
